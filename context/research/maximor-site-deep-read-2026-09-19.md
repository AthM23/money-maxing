## 2. Case studies

### Kiteworks — `/case-study/kiteworks` (all below from this page unless noted)

**Who.** Cybersecurity / secure content sharing. $1B+ valuation, 100M+ end users, 3,650+ enterprise and
government customers. **10 legal entities** after **6 acquisitions**. **US, EMEA, APAC.**
**7 currencies · 66 accounts · 20+ bank relationships.** ERP is **NetSuite**; also payroll platforms,
expense tools, email, spreadsheets. Revenue: not stated.

**The pain.** **20 accountants** manually pulled data from siloed systems and manually matched, classified
and reconciled transactions **following entity-specific rules**. Significant time went to "different
currencies and reporting practices" just to get trustworthy numbers. No standard process → audit trails
hard to produce. Cash position took real daily effort; slow granular forecasting delayed board and sponsor
reporting. **Transaction volumes are not stated anywhere on the page.**

**What Maximor did.** Connected systems with no migration; analysed historical bank activity and
transaction records to learn the reasoning behind past decisions; built a context layer; agents match and
post into NetSuite and flag unusual cases for manager oversight; the team explains edge cases in natural
language and agents improve; consolidates data, **translates currencies, eliminates intercompany
transactions**; produces a **daily cash view by currency, legal entity and region**; balance-sheet
reconciliations with full audit traceability.

**Numbers.** 98% straight-through on cash transactions · **<4 weeks** to go live · **15 FTEs** of effort
saved · **20 → 5** accountants on global cash management.

**What the 2% is — now sourced.** The case study only says unusual scenarios are flagged. The press is
explicit: the remaining 2% "are escalated when an agent meets a decision it has not seen before", and once
the team resolves it the system handles it next time (Sovereign Magazine; same wording at cfotech.news).
**That sentence is demo story #2, written by them.**

**Quoted: Kristine Radhakrishnan, VP, Global Controller, Kiteworks.** Five quotes; the two that matter —
treasury and cash "was managed by over 20 people working through nonstandard processes across different
countries", and they are "no longer looking to add more resources."

**Inference (strong).** The unnamed "global cybersecurity company" in the press — 10 entities, 7
currencies, 20+ banks, 20 → 5, four-week rollout — **is Kiteworks**. Every figure matches. The team's
intel file treats these as possibly-separate customers; they are one. **Do not double-count.**

**Not mentioned on this page:** bank fees, remittance advice, lockbox, payment processors, withholding tax,
revenue schedules, close calendar.

### HiBid — `/case-study/hibid`

**Who.** Auction marketplace + vertical software for auctioneers. **$2.5B+ GMV/yr**, 100K+ auctions/yr.
**4 revenue streams, 50+ sub-streams**, each on a different recognition schedule. Billing in **three modes:
advance, point-of-sale, arrears**. Systems: data warehouse, billing engine, **multiple payment processors**,
bank accounts. Entities, countries, currencies, banks: **not stated**.

**The pain.** Hundreds of thousands of line items exported per cycle into Excel — the workbook hit
**300 MB**. Cash arrives by **check, wire, ACH and multiple payment processors**, and someone worked out by
hand what was **collected, what was AR, what was in transit**. Close took **3 weeks**. A prior **NetSuite
integration failed** after "nearly three months and significant engineering support" on volume.

**Numbers.** Close **3 weeks → 5 days** · **99%+** order-to-cash accuracy · **50+ hours** reclaimed monthly.
**Quoted: Chris Stiegal, CFO, HiBid** — rev rec, billing and collections "spread across different systems,
which made them very manual and complicated."

## 3. Blog and resources

All 13 posts from `/blog`. **Eight of thirteen, all Aug–Sep 2026, are about usage-based/consumption
revenue for AI-native companies.** That is their current centre of gravity.

- **What we mean by autonomous finance** (`/blog/what-we-mean-by-autonomous-finance`, Aug 13 2026,
  **Ramnandan Krishnamurthy, Co-founder & CEO**) — the manifesto. Automation stalled because the work "was
  never in the tools"; policy lives in controllers' heads and analysts' inboxes; ERPs record judgment's
  output, never the judgment. Agents must execute, not recommend. Coins **"human runtime"**,
  **"audit-ready becomes a property"**, and the line to steal: it **"remembers the answer, so it never asks
  twice."** Also "99% that runs autonomously is table stakes."
- **The usage-based rev rec playbook** (`/guide/usage-based-rev-rec`, Sep 15 2026, **Shruthi
  Sathyanarayanan, CPA, ex-Big 4**, reviewed by Big 4 partners) — six ASC 606 judgment calls with
  citations: setup/fine-tuning fees (606-10-25-19), outcome-based pricing (32-5), principal-vs-agent on
  resold tokens (55-36), prepaid credits and breakage (55-46), **period-end unbilled usage cutoff**
  (55-18), commissions (ASC 340-40).
- **5 revenue checks every usage-based CFO should run** (`/blog/usage-based-revenue-checks-cfo`, Aug 21
  2026) — **the most operationally concrete page on the site.** Invoiced ≠ recognised. Billed $0.10/token in
  January, owes a **blended $0.06** once March volume lands → true-up. Prepaid: **$900 for $1,000** of
  credits. Committed **$120K/yr** floor + overage. Metering lag → accrual plus a **lookback check**; re-run
  if the gap is under **~0.5% of audit materiality**.
- **Can your usage-based rev rec survive an audit?** (`/blog/can-your-usage-based-revenue-recognition-service-survive-audit`,
  Aug 19 2026) — auditors cannot verify logic living in "a controller's head or spreadsheet formula with no
  trail". Names **knowledge concentration** as an audit finding. The trail must be a *byproduct* of
  recognition, not reconstructed at PBC time. Cutoff testing, breakage redemption history, modification
  treatment across CRM/billing/ERP.
- **The allocation problem in hybrid AI contracts** (`/blog/allocation-problem-hybrid-ai-contracts`, Sep 2
  2026) — allocating across platform fee + credits + overage + services. SSP methods, **material rights**,
  financing components, residual-approach misuse. No dollar examples.
- **The tax liability accumulating while nobody is looking** (`/blog/indirect-tax-usage-based-billing`,
  Sep 7 2026) — **Texas 80% taxable / 20% exempt**; WA taxable; CA and FL not. Nexus **$100K** most states,
  **$500K** CA/TX/NY. **Withholding tax on cross-border cloud fees treated as royalties or technical
  service fees, treaty-dependent.** VAT/GST reverse charge, EU OSS, two non-contradictory location proofs,
  escheatment 3–5 yrs.
- **Why your revenue forecast breaks when customers control the meter**
  (`/blog/revenue-forecast-breaks-customers-control-meter`, Sep 7 2026) — existing-customer usage, not
  pipeline, drives consumption revenue. Layered forecast: **committed floor / overage / breakage**. Cohort
  ramp and decay, usage velocity, dormancy thresholds, backtest accuracy.
- **Will finance reporting accuracy hold in production?** (`/blog/will-finance-reporting-accuracy-hold-in-production`,
  Dec 15 2025) — **86%** of CFOs hit inaccurate AI output (Wakefield, Oct 2025). Names the evidence set:
  **source lineage, policy reference attached to the entry, calculation log, review trail.** Multi-currency
  intercompany eliminations across 20 entities; read-only ERP until trust is established.
- **7 moves to win your AI finance transformation** (`/infographics-jujitsu`, Sep 15 2026) — Move 1: "Kill
  your worst spreadsheet first", with **bank recs** named as the canonical first win. Move 2: measure
  **"error-free automations"**. Move 7: tie to a board KPI — close time, audit prep hours, **exception rates**.

Five more, reference only, little demo value: **Metrics investors trust least** (Sep 2 2026 — ARR/RPO/NRR

## 4. Their philosophy, in their own vocabulary

**The problem.** Finance runs on "16+ disconnected tools", loses "1,500+ hours a month to reconciliations
and validations", takes **8 to 11 days** to close (`/why`). The deeper claim is the workshop thesis in
prose: the policy is written down nowhere and lives in heads and inboxes (autonomous-finance post).

**What agents should do.** "Processing, matching, reconciling, posting" — and derive policy "from how your
team already works, apply it, and bring a human in when a case falls outside it" (`/why`). Execute
end-to-end; never hand back "homework" (autonomous-finance post).

**What they are not.** "Not a chatbot. Not a replacement ERP" (workshop slide 3). No dashboards, no
recommendations-awaiting-action, no scripts; **"no rip-and-replace, no migration"** (`/`).

**Trust / audit.** "Every entry, reconciliation and workpaper ships with its audit trail built in"; "Every
action is logged, reviewable and reversible"; **"No customer's data is ever used to train another's
model"** (all `/why`). Every answer "cites its source" and you can "drill from any figure to the entry
behind it" (`/`, `/instant-answers-and-search`).

**Humans.** Pull the team in "only where judgment matters" (`/why`). Controller framing: **"You review.
Maximor prepares."** and **"Rules that write themselves"** — the platform learns from approvals and
rejections (`/controller`). Their three-phase verb set is **Map → Modernize → Memorize**, ending in
"muscle memory" (same page).

**Vocabulary to mirror.** *Audit-Ready Agents™* · *unified finance context layer* · *human runtime* ·
*no rip-and-replace* · *knows when to stop and ask* · *takes responsibility for the whole workflow* ·
*buying back your team's judgment* · *straight-through* · *exceptions come to you* · *audit-ready becomes a
property* · *the one balance that does not tie is raised, not buried* (`/revenue-automation`) ·
*never asks twice*.

**Policy naming convention** (home widget): `SHORT-PAY-01` (short ≤ $50 → auto-close) → `v2` (≤ $50 **or
≤ 5% freight**); `ACCRUE-01` (no invoice by Day 2 → accrue) → v2 (auto-accrue repeat vendors); `REV-REC-01`
(recognise ratably, 12 mo) → v2 (true-ups auto-billed ≤ $10k). Named examples: Acme Freight #4471 (−$38,
posted & closed), Byte Foods #7729 (−$495, awaiting you).

## 5. Pain points that recur, ranked

Tags: **(a)** cash application / bank rec · **(b)** judgment in heads and inboxes · **(c)** audit
defensibility and close speed.

1. **One payment ≠ one invoice, and small short-pays are matched by hand.** *(a)* Their own assessment asks
   it verbatim: does someone hand-match when a customer "sends one payment covering multiple invoices — or
   short-pays by a small amount" (`/close-assessment`). The home widget's flagship policy is `SHORT-PAY-01`.
   HiBid: cash by check/wire/ACH/processors with collected vs AR vs in-transit derived by hand.
   **Most concrete, most repeated — and they scripted the demo beat themselves.**
2. **Cash across many entities, currencies, banks and country rules.** *(a)* Kiteworks: 10 entities, 7
   currencies, 66 accounts, 20+ banks, US/EMEA/APAC, **entity-specific rules**, currency translation,
   intercompany elimination. Echoed by the $220M/14-entity and $180M/22-entity examples (bandwidth post).
3. **The reason lives in one person's head or inbox.** *(b)* "If your controller were unreachable for an
   entire close cycle, would the close suffer" (`/close-assessment`); **knowledge concentration** as an
   audit finding (audit post); the 2% that is handled automatically next time; Dura's "buying back your
   team's judgment".
4. **Workpapers assembled from spreadsheets, emails and shared drives at audit time.** *(c)* >1 day to
   assemble (`/close-assessment`); PBC scramble down 80% (accuracy post); audit findings 7 → 0 (roll-up);
   Rently "audit issues by over 90%".
5. **Clearing/suspense balances grow month over month**, and bank rec happens in the last days of close
   rather than continuously. *(a/c)* `/close-assessment`.
6. **Adding an entity by acquisition raises days-to-close for at least two quarters.** *(a/c)* Same page;
   Kiteworks' 6 acquisitions → 10 entities.
7. **Contract → schedule → GL not traceable in 15 minutes; mid-quarter modifications reworked by hand in
   spreadsheets.** *(c)* Same page; audit post.
8. **Accruals and prepaids run from spreadsheets someone must remember to reverse.** *(b/c)* Same page.

## 6. What a non-generic demo would contain

Twelve realistic details for a simulated mid-market SaaS July. Stories: **(1)** cash application that knows
when it does not know · **(2)** the reason lives in somebody's inbox · **(3)** an entry you can defend at audit.

1. **One wire covering three invoices, short by a freight-sized amount** — `SHORT-PAY-01` auto-closes −$38,
   escalates −$495. Home widget + close-assessment Q5. → **(1)(2)**
2. **Receipts in 3–4 currencies across 2–3 entities and 6–8 bank accounts**, with a daily cash view *by
   currency, entity and region*. Kiteworks. → **(1)**
3. **A customer pays from a different legal entity than the one that invoiced** — Kiteworks' 10 entities
   from 6 acquisitions, "entity-specific rules"; needs an intercompany elimination. → **(1)(3)**
4. **An international wire short by an intermediary bank deduction** (€40,000 invoiced, €39,972 received).
   *Inference* from 20+ bank relationships across EMEA/APAC — **Maximor never names bank fees anywhere.** → **(1)**
5. **A customer in a treaty country deducts withholding tax at source** on a cloud fee treated as a royalty.
   The indirect-tax post names exactly this risk. → **(1)(3)**
6. **A processor payout that nets fees and covers many invoices** — HiBid's "multiple payment processors"
   and its manual collected/in-transit/AR split. → **(1)**
7. **Remittance advice arriving separately by email**, so the match needs the inbox. *Inference* — not named
   on the site, though Gmail is a named policy-learning source (`/why`). → **(1)(2)**
8. **A realised FX difference** between invoice-date and receipt-date rates. Kiteworks currency translation;
   `/board-ready-reporting` "FX rates apply automatically". → **(3)**
9. **A suspense/clearing balance that grows until someone cleans it up** before close can finish.
   Close-assessment Q4. → **(1)(3)**
10. **A usage true-up**: invoiced $0.10/token in July, blended rate falls to $0.06 once quarterly volume
    lands → true-up plus a lookback against the prior accrual. 5-revenue-checks post. → **(3)**
11. **A prepaid credit pack ($900 for $1,000)** whose deferred balance must tie to the remaining credit
    pool. Same post. → **(3)**
12. **A mid-quarter contract modification** that CRM knows about and the ERP revenue schedule does not.
    Close-assessment Q2 + audit post. → **(3)**

**Best credibility per unit of build effort — do these three:**

- **#1, the multi-invoice short-pay plus the ask-once loop.** Zero new engine work: tier-0 → kernel →
  ledger already exists, and Maximor scripted the beat on their own home page and asks it as
  close-assessment Q5. Land the `v2` version bump on screen.
- **#2 + #3, multi-currency / multi-entity / paid-by-the-wrong-entity.** **A seed-data change only** — no
  new logic, just entities, currencies and bank accounts in the fixtures. Highest-value single edit,
  because it converts the demo from "a SaaS with one bank account" into the Kiteworks shape, which is what
  the judges' flagship case study is about.
- **#9, a suspense balance that clears only because the agent partitioned the unknown.** Cheap, and it makes
  "knows when it does not know" visible as a *balance* rather than a claim.

Skip #4 and #7 if time is short: both are inferences, and a Maximor judge may notice the site never claims them.

## 7. Gaps and cautions

- **Conflict — Dura vs the unnamed roll-up.** `/get-started` shows "30+ subsidiaries" and "7→0 audit
  findings" beside Sloan Session's Dura quote, but the press attributes 14 business units / 30+
  subsidiaries / 7→0 findings to an **unnamed** PE-backed roll-up (GlobeNewswire), while `/partnerships`
  gives Dura as **$150M revenue, PE-backed** with no subsidiary count. **Do not say Dura has 30+
  subsidiaries or 7→0 audit findings.**
- **The 98% has two different scopes.** Kiteworks page: 98% straight-through **on cash transactions**.
  Press: ~98% **of transactions on the platform**, company-wide. Say which one you mean.
- **Automation-rate claims are inconsistent across pages** — marketing tiers, not one measurement: 90%
  (`/`), 98%+ (`/cash-automation`), 95%+ and 90%+ (`/offer`), 85%+ (`/cfo-offer-all`), 70–80% (bandwidth
  post). Accuracy likewise: 99%+, 99.7%, "99%+ validated". **None of these belongs on a slide as *the*
  number.**
- **Rently 11→3 is the site's figure** (also `/controller`, `/offer`); TechCrunch's 8→4 is still
  unreconciled, but the site is at least internally consistent.
- **"$2M+ returned per $100M of revenue"** (`/`) has no methodology anywhere. Attribute it to them out loud
  or drop it.
- **The CFO benchmark is Wakefield Research, 100 CFOs, $50M–$500M ARR** (`/cfo-benchmark`), but the Dec 2025
  posts date the fieldwork to **October 2025** while the benchmark page shows Sep 15 2026. One survey,
  republished — do not present 96/14/97 as fresh 2026 data.
- **Security page unreadable** (trust.maximor.ai, 403). The no-cross-customer-training line is sourced to
  `/why`, which is enough; do not attribute it to a trust portal nobody read.
- **Where the team's framing may be off.** Three things. (i) **Kiteworks is not a simple SaaS** — a seed
  world of one entity, one currency, one bank account cannot carry the story the judges' own flagship case
  study tells; §6 items 2 and 3 fix that cheaply. (ii) **Eight of their thirteen blog posts, all from the
  last six weeks, are about usage-based revenue for AI-native companies** — that is where their thinking
  currently lives, and a demo company billed on metered usage (the $0.10→$0.06 blended true-up, the
  $900-for-$1,000 credit pack) would mirror their newest work rather than their 2025 positioning.
  (iii) **`/close-assessment` is a ten-question list of the exact pains they sell against** — treat it as a
  checklist for the demo script: every question it asks should be answerable "yes, and here is where we
  handle it."
