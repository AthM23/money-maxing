Maximor PDF · 38 pages · read in full ✓✓

The Usage-Based Revenue Recognition Playbook

An ASC 606 practitioner guide by Shruthi Sathyanarayanan, Founding Strategist at Maximor, written for AI-native companies that charge per token, per API call or per resolved outcome. Six controller questions, each with the accounting theory, a scenario with real numbers, a decision tree and an operational fix. The last three pages matter most to us: Maximor's own account of how its agents learn and when they escalate.

"The accounting is knowable — your team can reach the right answer on any single contract. What breaks down is execution at scale… while producing the audit trail that proves your conclusions were deliberate, not accidental."Page 35, "How Maximor Closes the Execution Gap". Our thesis, in their words.

Self-learning

"Every recommendation traces to a specific authoritative paragraph or a precedent in your policy library — never an invented cite."
What we have

Every judgment amount must cite an approved policy, an active fact or a quoted source filed under that party. The kernel re-reads the quote character by character before anything posts.

What we add

"Never an invented cite" is a promise. Ours is enforced by code with no model in it, and shown live by changing one character.

Self-improving

"…the platform learns the underlying pattern, not just the one-off correction. The same mistake is never made twice. Override rates fall from ~30% in month one to under 5% by month six."
What we have

The same curve, measured, on one month run three times: 12 approvals → 6 → 0, and 24 model calls → 0.

What we add

Generalising a pattern from a single override is the exact risk a scope guards against. With us a one-off answer stays one-off; a pattern needs three agreeing cases, a backtest, and a person's approval before it does anything. Say it as a complement, not a criticism.

Self-escalating

"An escalation is a package: the provision that triggered it, the candidate conclusions, why it couldn't resolve, and the relevant cites and prior memos."
What we have

The Slack card lists what was checked with hit counts, and carries the old answer when one has lapsed.

What to change

Lay the card out in their four parts: trigger, candidate treatments, why it could not resolve, sources. Cheap, and the judges will recognise it. Note the honest ratio too: "~80%" handled and "~20%" escalated for revenue contracts, against 99/1 on their revenue page and 98/2 in press. Partitioning beats an auto-clear number; in our corpus ESCALATE is the largest route (39 of 115).

What to take from it

  • Copy the format, not the topic. Every section opens with the controller's question in the first person, then a scenario with specific numbers, then a decision tree, then an operational fix. Open each act of the demo the same way. It matches their close assessment, so this is plainly how Maximor likes a story told.
  • Stay out of ASC 606. Five of the six questions need a revenue engine we do not have, and the person who wrote this guide may be in the room. Only section 05 is in reach.
  • Section 05 is buildable, and triple-anchored. "This is the one that lands on the close calendar every single month." Accrue delivered-but-unbilled usage from the recent daily run-rate, reverse it when the invoice generates, track estimate against actual: "a persistent bias in that variance is an audit flag". It lines up with the homepage's ACCRUE-01 rule and assessment question 8, and it is deterministic, which suits the kernel.
  • Who they are courting. AI-native, usage-priced companies; seven of their thirteen blog posts say the same. A Northwind that bills usage in arrears and sells prepaid credits looks like their pipeline. It also gives act one a SaaS-shaped short-pay reason: a customer disputing metered overage. ∴
  • They name the enemy for us. "Rules engines break when judgment is required. Spreadsheets do not learn. And generic AI hallucinates its way through the Codification."
  • A line for act three. On outcome-based pricing they say you need "an auditable, immutable record… it is exactly what an auditor will vouch." That is a decision trace.

White space · no vendor found claiming it

Where Footnote is alone

  • Memory that is scoped, expires, and is capped at the answerer's approval limit. Every learning claim we found is unscoped.
  • A kernel that re-verifies quoted evidence and rejects on a one-character change. Nearest: Rillet's frozen snapshots.
  • Autonomy earned from measured accuracy, per kind of entry. Campfire's dial moves "as your comfort grows".
  • A stated re-run with zero model calls and a backtest per policy.
  • An investigator across Gmail, Slack, contracts and CRM for one short-pay. Deduction tools are built for retail evidence (proof of delivery, trade promotions); nobody does SaaS-shaped deductions such as SLA credits or seat true-downs.
  • An auditor fenced off from preparer memory, with the reviewer on a different model.
  • A reproducible public evaluation of the vendor's own agent.
Do not pitch these as unique

Already common

  • Duplicate-invoice detection (Stampli, Ramp, BILL, AppZen)
  • Slack approve and reject buttons (Campfire, Light)
  • Building an agent from a finished close and testing it against approved numbers: FloQast Transform launched 16 Sep 2026; Ledge compiles a working paper into code. Our difference is compiling from individual judgments, with a published backtest.
  • Confidence thresholds, flux narratives, bill coding
  • "Asks in Slack and learns" (Maximor itself)

Consolidated take

What I would do, and the nine scenes worth arguing about

Everything above, boiled down. The avenues are spines; these are the individual scenes that could go on screen, each tied to a named Maximor source, with the case for and against.

The shape of the problem

  • The judges asked for real customers' pains, but no Maximor customer story goes below outcome level. So split the job three ways: customers supply the stakes (a named quote on a title card), Maximor's own mock-ups and assessment questions supply the scenario, and specialist vendors' documentation supplies the realistic detail.
  • The best single finding is also the biggest risk: their homepage and question 5 are our demo. Done lazily it reads as a copy. Done well it reads as "we took your screenshot seriously and built what comes after it".
  • What comes after it is four things nobody claims: an answer with a scope, judgment compiled into a policy with a backtest, a zero-model-call re-run, and a kernel that verifies evidence. Every scene should land on one of those four or it is filler.

A test for "not generic"

  • A source line on screen: Maximor's own sentence or a named customer's quote, not a statistic.
  • A specific amount, document and person: INV-1042, $1,200 short, the CEO's email of 28 June, Dana the controller. Never "an invoice".
  • A moment it hurts: day three of close, the auditor's request, the customer who gets dunned for an invoice they paid.
  • A case where the easy answer is wrong. The strategic short-payer, the lapsed one-time answer, the edited email. Generic demos only show the happy path.

What I would not lead with

  • "It asks in Slack and remembers." Maximor says that already. Lead with the scope.
  • Breadth. One closing line, as the judges said.
  • The fine-tune making cash application "smarter". Pitch cost, latency and routing for extraction, with the benchmark number, including the unflattering rows.
  • Anything in ASC 606. Their strategist wrote the 38-page guide.

Where the hours should go

  • Rehearse the spine for real. The live model run is n = 1, the Slack round trip has never been run, and the console has never been opened in a browser. These are a bigger risk than any missing feature.
  • Build remittance capture if it fits before the freeze. It completes question 5 and puts the fine-tuned extractor inside the story.
  • Re-price the seed so one shortfall sits under $50 and one near $495, and add the must-not-fire counter-example.
  • Re-lay the escalation card in Maximor's four-part package.
  • Record a fallback take of every live moment.
Bottom line

Five scenes, one month, one company: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5

Scenes 1, 3, 4 and 5 already run and are the recommended core. Scene 2 is the one build worth adding. Kiteworks appears as the stakes line that opens scene 1, and, only if the team chooses to widen scope, as the single foreign-currency receipt in scene 6. Everything else is a closing line or stays out.
